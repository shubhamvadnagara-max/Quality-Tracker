5 most important things

    How to read this page

    What every number above means and how it is worked out. All of it comes from Dynamic's inspection reports, for the filters at the top.

    Lot
    One Dynamic inspection report: one style, buyer and offer.
    Lots passed
    Lots Dynamic passed, out of all lots. Failed and re-offered lots count as not passed.
    Pieces checked
    The pieces in Dynamic's sample (for example 80 or 125), added up over all lots.
    Defects per 100 pieces
    All defects found ÷ pieces checked × 100. Example: 136 defects in 1,322 pieces = 10.3 per 100. Lower is better.
    AQL limit
    For each sample the buyer allows a number of major and a number of minor defects. One defect more than allowed fails the lot.
    At the limit, room left
    At the limit: the lot used every defect allowed, so one more would have failed it. Room left: at least one more defect was still allowed. Over: more defects than allowed.
    Major and minor
    A major defect can make a garment unsellable or come back from the buyer; a minor one is small. Far fewer majors are allowed, so majors fail lots first.
    Measurement problems
    Lots where Dynamic wrote a size outside the buyer's tolerance. Lines such as "within tolerance" or "see attached sheet" are not counted.
    Clean checks
    Lots with no stitching or finishing defect at all.
    Department
    Who caused a defect, as Dynamic wrote it. When Dynamic leaves it blank it comes from the defect type, for example uncut thread is finishing. "Not clear" means it could not be told.
    Report card score
    Out of 100. Lots passed 10: share of lots passed. Defects per 100 pieces 30: 30 × (1 − defects per 100 ÷ 100), so 10.3 per 100 keeps 26.9 points. Kept away from the AQL limit 20: share of lots with room left. Sizes in tolerance 10: share of lots with no measurement problem.
    Major and total defects on the card
    Major defects 10: the average of three shares: majors per 100 pieces, the allowed majors used, and the defects that were major. Total defects 20: 20 × (1 − all defects found ÷ all defects allowed), for lots where both limits were read.
    Grades
    A 85 and up, B 70 to 84, C 55 to 69, D 40 to 54, F below 40. With fewer than 3 lots the card says "few lots yet".
    5 most important things
    Every risk the tracker knows is checked: failed lots, lots over or at the limit, the biggest defect, majors, the department with most defects, size problems, change from last month, buyers and reports to check. They are ranked by how close they bring lots to failing, and the top 5 are shown with their numbers.
    Week by week
    Weeks start on Monday. Each bar is that week's defects per 100 pieces; under it are its lots and pieces checked.
    Check
    A report marked Check has something to confirm, such as an AQL limit that could not be read. Its numbers count, but correct them in All reports.

    DHU tracker

    Our own 100% checks. DHU is defects found per 100 pieces checked. Lower is better.

    How to read the DHU numbers

    What every DHU number above means and how it is worked out, from your own stitching and finishing sheets.

    DHU
    Defects per hundred units: defects found ÷ pieces checked × 100. 11.6% means about 12 defects in every 100 pieces checked. One piece can have more than one defect.
    Stitching DHU
    From the end-of-line check on each stitching line: what the line's own checkers found.
    Finishing DHU
    From the 100% check in finishing. It also includes faults made earlier, in stitching, cutting or fabric, that earlier checks missed.
    Stitching faults found in finishing
    Stitching faults the finishing check still found, per 100 pieces it checked. Each of these got past the stitching check. Lower is better.
    Finishing's own DHU
    Only the defects finishing causes itself: uncut thread, pressing and contamination.
    Sent back from finishing
    Pieces checked minus pieces passed, as a share of pieces checked: the pieces that went back for repair.
    Where defects come from
    Each defect name belongs to one department, for example open seam to stitching and stains to handling. The list is in DHU data, where it can be changed.
    Stitching lines
    Line numbers as written in the sheets. A line with under 200 pieces checked is marked "few", because a few pieces can make its DHU look much better or worse than it is.
    Styles letting most stitching faults through
    Only styles with 300 pieces or more at the finishing check, so that a small run cannot top the list. Faults per 100 pieces, worst first.
    The quality chain
    Defects found at each check, per 100 pieces checked there: stitching, then finishing, then Dynamic. What a later check still finds was missed by the checks before it.
    5 most important things
    The biggest risks of the month: stitching faults reaching finishing, the worst line, the worst style, finishing's own and stain defects, pieces sent back, change from last month and mistakes in the sheets. When the same risk shows in more than one factory, the others are added under "Also".
    Compared with last month
    ▲ is higher than last month and ▼ lower. Green is better, red is worse.
